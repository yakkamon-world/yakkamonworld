Was The Reward Ladder Nerfed? An Honest Look At The Numbers

Aug 12, 2026
▶ Prefer to watch? The full analysis, 14 minutes ↗

We’ve published the new ladder. This is the analysis: who gained, who lost, whether “nothing has been nerfed” holds up, and one consequence we don’t think anyone has spotted yet.

Short version: the team’s claim is defensible on utility and hard to defend on scarcity, the middle of the leaderboard took the hit, and the change does nothing at all about the biggest structural problem in the reward design.

1. The same number of eggs, half the Legendaries

This is the cleanest way to see what happened. Total eggs awarded barely moved. The composition changed enormously.

EggBeforeAfterChange
Legendary A10050−50

Legendary C500257−243
All Legendaries1,000510−490
Rare Egg A4,0001,500−2,500
Rare Egg B—3,000+3,000
Total eggs5,0005,010+10

Ten more eggs go out than before — the extra ones handed to the top 10. But Legendary supply is roughly halved, and the Rare tier was split into two grades where there used to be one.

If you hold a Legendary, this update was unambiguously good for you. Halving the supply of something tradable while demand stays flat is the textbook definition of making it scarcer. That’s worth stating plainly, because most of the commentary has focused on the people who dropped a band.

2. The middle took the hit

The bands didn’t compress evenly. Here’s what a given finishing rank earns now against what it earned before.

Ten ranks improved. Roughly 3,800 ranks moved down a grade. Around 1,200 were untouched.

The sharpest single change is ranks 501–1,000, which fell out of the Legendary bracket altogether. Five hundred trainers who were planning around a Legendary Egg C are now planning around a Rare Egg A.

The awkward part is timing. Deposits opened on 9 August with a multiplier that rewards moving early. This ladder changed on 12 August. Anyone who deposited in the opening week did so while aiming at a target that has since moved — and the multiplier that made early deposits attractive is the same mechanism that made it impossible to wait and see.

3. Does “nothing has been nerfed” hold up?

The team’s statement is precise, and it’s worth reading it precisely: this update does not nerf an existing Yakkamon for a leaderboard ranking. Lower-tier utility is being lifted, and more monsters are being released.

Taken literally, that’s accurate. No monster got weaker. If Rare Egg B hatches something more useful than Rare Egg A would have last week, a rank-3,000 trainer genuinely does end up with a better creature than the old ladder would have given them.

But the sentence answers a question about power, and most of the concern is about scarcity. Those aren’t the same thing, and in a game with tradable on-chain assets, scarcity is half of what a reward is worth.

ClaimOur read
No monster was made weakerFair, and probably true
Lower-tier utility is being liftedPlausible, but unverifiable until September
More monsters are being releasedFair — more species, though not more eggs
Therefore nothing was nerfedDoesn’t follow. Halving Legendary supply is a change in what a rank is worth, whatever happens to utility

Our position: this is a rebalance with winners and losers, not a nerf and not a pure upgrade. Describing it as neither would have cost the team nothing and been more accurate.

4. The thing nobody has mentioned

Here’s the part we think matters most, and it’s a consequence rather than an intention.

We wrote last week about a wallet-splitting problem in the deposit design: because the size bonus is rated per deposit and the rewards are granted per account, a large depositor can split across wallets, lose about 10% of their points, and multiply their reward slots.

The new ladder does not fix this. The double-egg reward is granted per account, so a splitter collects it on every wallet they run.

Same money, opening weekOld ladderNew ladder
One wallet at rank 11 egg2 eggs
Ten wallets across ranks 1–1010 eggs20 eggs
Advantage of splitting10×10×

The ratio is unchanged. Concentration got twice as good, and so did splitting — so the relative incentive to run multiple wallets is exactly where it was, while the absolute prize for doing so doubled.

That assumes a splitter’s wallets each carry enough points to occupy the top ranks, which for a large depositor in the opening week is a reasonable assumption rather than a certainty.

What would actually address it. Not a steeper curve — we suggested that previously and it doesn’t work here, because A, B and C are the same power level by design and steepening would break that. The available fix is the team’s own precedent: require verified Discord, verified X and a minimum of non-deposit points for reward eligibility, exactly as they already do for referrals. Ten split wallets would then each need aged accounts and a week of daily play.

5. What we still don’t know

One number decides whether this update was good or bad for you, and it hasn’t been published.

If the utility gap between Legendary Egg C and Rare Egg A is small, then ranks 501–1,000 lost very little and the team’s framing is right. If it’s large, five hundred trainers lost a great deal on three days’ notice. Monster utilities are due in September, and until then anyone telling you confidently which way this went — in either direction — is guessing.

Two other open questions: the update covered reward eggs only, so the early-access wave bands stand as previously published; and it’s not stated whether the double eggs at the top are two separate creatures or a single reward described in two parts.

Our verdict

  • Top 10: a clear win. Two eggs where there was one.
  • Ranks 11–50 and 101–250: unchanged, and now sitting on a scarcer asset. Quietly the best outcome on the board.
  • Ranks 251–1,000: the real losers, and 501–1,000 most of all.
  • Ranks 1,001–5,000: broadly neutral. Rare Egg B instead of Rare Egg A, and nobody yet knows what that means.
  • Outside 5,000: unaffected. Early access still runs to rank 100,000.

The change itself is defensible. Concentrating rewards at the very top and extending them deeper into the field is a reasonable way to make the ladder feel less flat, and the top 100 being uniformly identical was a genuine design weakness.

What we’d push back on is the framing. “Nothing has been nerfed” is a claim about power in an update that was mostly about scarcity, and it arrived three days into a deposit window that penalises waiting. Saying “we’ve rebalanced this, here’s who it affects” would have been both more honest and, we think, better received.

We’ll update this piece when the September utilities land. If they show the gap is small, we’ll say so as plainly as we’ve said this.
