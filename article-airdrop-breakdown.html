What Actually Changed in the Airdrop

Aug 14, 2026 · Odds updated for a field of 77,763 trainers
Update, 2 September 2026. Two of the names used below have changed. The official Yakkapedia and airdrop table now run Storm / Echo / Ghost / Bloom: the 251–500 monster this article calls Bloom is now Ghost, and the 501–2,000 monster it calls Tide is now Bloom. The team says the monsters and their boosts are unchanged — the names were swapped to match their types. The utilities are also published now: Storm makes Yakkamon within range work 2× as fast (large AOE), Echo doubles the resources of those within range (small AOE), Ghost keeps those within range at maximum stamina (small AOE), and Bloom regenerates a share of its Max HP every combat turn. Total supplies are 75 / 275 / 400 / 2,000, with 68 of them hidden in the 14 September free mint. The rank bands below are unchanged. The full table →

Yakkamon rewrote the Monster NFT airdrop on 12 August and published the monster names on the 14th. Between the two, the picture only became legible this week — and it’s a bigger change than the first announcement suggested.

This is the full breakdown: what moved, the supply maths, your actual odds, and where the change creates problems nobody is discussing.

1. The mapping that explains everything

The old ladder used placeholder labels. The new one uses monster names, and one line in the team’s own comparison table is the key to reading the whole change:

“Echo is the old Legendary A.” Bloom was Legendary Egg B. Tide was Legendary Egg C. And Storm is entirely new — it was added above the existing ladder rather than inserted into it.

Once you have that, every band becomes comparable:

1,200 ranks improved. 3,800 held their slot with utility lifted. Zero moved down. That’s the whole change in one line, and it’s why the team’s “nothing has been nerfed” claim survives scrutiny.

2. The supply maths

BeforeAfterChange
Genesis Legendaries1,0002,010+1,010
Rare Eggs4,0003,000−1,000
Total Monster NFTs5,0005,010+10

The individual counts are official: Storm 50, Echo 203, Bloom 257, Tide 1,500. The airdrop totals 5,010 Monster NFTs across 5,000 trainers — the extra ten are the second monsters going to ranks 1–10.

The composition shift is the striking part. Legendaries went from 20% of all airdropped NFTs to 40%. The same number of trainers are rewarded; twice the proportion receive a Legendary.

Nobody was diluted by this. Worth stating plainly, because “doubling supply” normally means existing holders lose out. Here nothing has been minted yet — the airdrop happens at early access. Doubling the supply of an asset nobody holds costs nobody anything. It simply moves a thousand trainers from Rare to Legendary.

3. Your actual odds

So the practical effect for an individual trainer is that the chance of finishing with a Legendary roughly doubled, from about one in 78 to about one in 39.

And you can now watch this decay in real time. We first ran these numbers against a field of 68,700 trainers. The field has grown 13% in the days since, and every one of these percentages fell with it — a Legendary slipped from 2.91% to 2.57% without a single rule changing. The reward bands are fixed in number; the queue for them is not. That is the argument for climbing now rather than later, and it gets stronger every day you wait.

4. Storm changes the design, not just the ladder

Until this week the site’s position — taken from the team’s own statements — was that the Genesis Legendaries are equal in power and differ in species and role rather than strength. Some battle monsters, some farm utility.

Storm is the stated exception. It’s described as the newest monster in the set and the hardest to earn, and the only route to one is a top-50 finish.

That matters beyond the reward table, because it fixes something structural. Under the old ladder, ranks 1 through 100 all received an identical reward — meaning the difference between finishing first and finishing ninety-ninth was worth precisely nothing. We flagged that as a real design weakness after the first dev stream.

Adding a scarcer monster above the ladder solves it without breaking the rest. The team couldn’t steepen the curve by making Echo beat Bloom — that would contradict the same-power-level design the other three rest on. Adding a fourth monster above them creates the gradient while leaving that design intact. It’s a neat solution and worth crediting.

5. The leaderboard locks — and that’s the part to plan around

New terminology arrived with this update, and it’s the most consequential thing in it for anyone currently sitting in a good position.

The team’s wording: you have to earn your rank and hold it until the leaderboard locks. Once it does, the airdrop is closed for good.

  • A rank you occupy today is not a rank you own. Sitting at 1,800 now means nothing if two hundred people pass you before the lock.
  • There is no second chance. No later window, no follow-up airdrop, and — the team is explicit — no sale and no way to buy in.
  • The lock is now dated relative to launch. The official Important Dates page puts it one week before early access, with rewards three days later — and early access itself is now given as November or December. The exact day is still to be announced. The full schedule →
Combine that with the hidden leaderboard and a common plan stops working. The team said on the first dev stream that the board will be hidden as launch approaches, to stop last-day gaming. So “coast now, sprint at the end” fails twice over: you won’t know where the line is, and you won’t know whether you’ve crossed it.

6. Where climbing actually pays

Most of this ladder is flat, and points spent inside a band buy nothing at all.

Climbing from rank 1,900 to rank 600 gains you nothing — both receive Tide. Climbing from 2,100 to 1,999 moves you from a Rare Egg to a Genesis Legendary. The value is entirely at the edges: ranks 10, 50, 250, 500, 2,000, 5,000 and 100,000.

The widened Tide band is a double-edged thing. It made a Legendary far more reachable — and it created the largest flat zone in the Legendary bracket, 1,500 ranks deep. If you land inside it, further effort is wasted unless you can realistically reach 500.

7. The problem this update makes worse

One consequence nobody has raised, and it follows directly from the widening.

We wrote previously about a wallet-splitting incentive in the deposit design: because the deposit size bonus is rated per deposit and the rewards are granted per account, a large depositor can split across wallets, lose around 10% of their points, and multiply their reward slots.

Extending the Legendary band to rank 2,000 makes that more attractive, not less. More split wallets now land inside Legendary territory.

Wallets occupying the top ranksOld ladderNow
11 monster2 monsters
1010 monsters20 monsters, all Legendary
100100 monsters110 monsters, all Legendary

That assumes each split wallet carries enough points to occupy a top rank, which for a large depositor in the opening deposit week is plausible rather than certain. But the direction is unambiguous.

The fix is the team’s own precedent. They already require the friend to link a wallet and deposit 100 $FLOWER before a referral counts. Applying the same test to airdrop eligibility — requiring a minimum of points earned from non-deposit sources — would make a hundred split wallets need a hundred aged accounts and a week of daily play each. It costs honest players nothing.

8. What this tells you about the team

Three things worth reading from how this was handled.

They expanded rather than reshuffled. The easy version of this update would have moved rewards around within a fixed pool. Instead they added a monster and widened the Legendary bracket — that costs them scarcity, and they did it anyway.

They published a before-and-after table. Most studios announce the new state and let players work out what moved. Publishing a band-by-band comparison, including the bands that only held their slot, is a choice that invites scrutiny.

But the naming came two days late, and that gap caused real confusion — including ours. The 12 August table used labels like “Rare Egg A” for a band that turns out to receive a Genesis Legendary. We read that as a downgrade and published an analysis that was wrong. Announcing the names and the bands together would have prevented every misreading we’ve seen this week.

9. What’s still unknown

  • The utilities. Due in September. Everything about what these monsters actually do — and what “utility lifted” amounts to in practice — is unverifiable until then.
  • The lock date. Not announced. It decides how long you have.
  • Whether the two monsters at ranks 1–10 are separate creatures or one reward described in two parts. The phrasing points at separate, and the 5,010-across-5,000 total supports that.
  • Where Storm sits against the others in practice. “Hardest to earn” is a statement about scarcity. Whether it’s also stronger is a September question.

The short version

  • Legendary supply doubled — 1,000 to 2,010, now 40% of all airdropped NFTs.
  • The Legendary line moved out from rank 1,000 to rank 2,000. If you’d written a Legendary off, reconsider.
  • Nobody moved down a band. 1,200 ranks improved, 3,800 held their slot.
  • Storm is new, top 50 only, and it gives the leaderboard a gradient it badly needed.
  • You have to hold your rank until the leaderboard locks — and you won’t be able to see the line when it happens.
  • The wallet-splitting hole got wider, and the fix already exists in their referral rules.
