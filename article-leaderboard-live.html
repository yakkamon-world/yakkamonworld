The Whole Deposit Board Is Now Public — And It Isn’t Full

Aug 19, 2026

There is a new tab on the portal. Leaderboard ranks every $FLOWER deposit made on Base and Ronin, scored on the published point rules, and lets you find your own position in it. It also works out what the reward you are chasing would cost you today, and what the same reward costs if you leave it a month.

None of it comes from anyone’s word for it. Deposits are transfers on public blockchains, so they can be read directly, added up and ranked. That is all the page does.

And the first thing it shows is not a ranking at all. It is a count.

Fewer addresses have deposited than there are prizes

At the time of writing, a little over 3,000 deposit addresses have ever received $FLOWER across both chains. The Genesis airdrop has 5,000 places.

Read carefully, because this is easy to overstate. It does not mean there are only 3,000 people in the race — there are hundreds of thousands of pre-registered trainers, and most of them have never deposited a thing. What it means is narrower and more useful: on deposit points alone, the board does not yet reach 5,000 names. The Tide band at rank 2,000 is full. The Rare Egg band at 5,000 is not, and cannot be, until more people deposit than have.

That is why the reward bands on the page read “Not full yet” rather than showing a number. There is no honest line to quote for a band nobody has filled.

The practical reading: the top of the real leaderboard is settled by money, because deposits scale in a way nothing else does. The bottom half of the reward zone is settled by everyone else — streaks, referrals and quests, none of which touch a blockchain.

What the page actually contains

  • The full ranking. Every depositing address, its points, its $FLOWER total, how many separate deposits it made, and whether it used Base, Ronin or both. Paged a hundred at a time, with a jump-to-rank box.
  • Find your rank. Paste your deposit address and the page highlights your row, fills in your points, and tells you where you sit.
  • Plan your climb. Pick the reward you want, say how safe you want to play it, and it works out the deposit that would take you there, what that costs at the live $FLOWER price, and what the same target costs at a later multiplier.
  • What each prize takes. The current points line for every band from Storm at the top to the Rare Egg at 5,000, with the deposit needed to reach it from a standing start this week.
The address to paste is not your wallet. It is the deposit address Yakkamon issued to you — the same one on both chains. Open your account, scroll to QUESTS, click DEPOSIT on the early-deposit row, and keep scrolling: it sits near the bottom of that panel. It only ever receives your own deposits, so sharing it gives nothing away.

The formula, checked against a real account

points = amount × (week multiplier + bulk bonus)

The two bonuses add. They do not multiply, which is the single most common mistake in threads about this. At 2.8×, a 500 $FLOWER deposit earns 1,400 points plus a 100 bonus — the bonus is 20% of the deposit, not 20% of the points.

Before publishing any of this we tested it the only way that settles the question: we took a real account with a known score, rebuilt its deposits from chain data, and ran them through the formula. Five transfers, all in the opening week at 3.0×. The calculation returned 71,217 points. The game showed the same account 71,217 points. Not close — identical.

So the numbers on the page are not an approximation of the official scoring. They are the official scoring, applied to public data.

One transfer is worth more than ten

The bulk bonus is rated on each transfer separately: +10% at 50, +20% at 500, +40% at 5,000, +80% at 50,000. We have been saying for two weeks that this makes one large early deposit worth considerably more than the same money dribbled in. The board is the first place you can see somebody actually do it.

One address on the ranking shows 50,200 $FLOWER and just over 190,000 points. Only one shape of deposit produces that total: a single 50,000 transfer in the opening week, catching the 80% band at 3.0×. Split into ten transfers of 5,000 the same money would have paid roughly 170,000. Split into a hundred transfers of 500 it would have paid about 160,000.

Splitting costs you a little. Waiting costs you more than half.

What this board does not count

This is the important caveat, and it is on the page itself rather than buried here.

Nurture streaks and referral points do not exist on any blockchain. They live in Yakkamon’s own database, where nobody outside the team can see them. So every score on our board is a floor: everyone’s real total is higher, and the official ranking will differ from ours.

How much it differs depends on where you look. A trainer who has nurtured daily since launch and referred a handful of friends might be carrying somewhere north of a thousand points that we cannot see — enough to leap over a great many small depositors, and nowhere near enough to matter at the top, where single addresses hold six figures. Our board is therefore a good picture of the top of the race, a rough picture of the middle, and a poor one of the tail.

Treat these lines as a floor, not a verdict. If the page puts you at rank 900, your real rank is probably better than 900 and definitely not worse.

The clock behind all of it

The week multiplier started at 3.0× and steps down 0.2× every week to a 1.0× floor from 19 October. Windows turn at 02:00 UTC, not midnight, which is worth knowing if you are timing a transfer against a deadline. The last window closes at 01:59 UTC on 16 November, and after that deposits stop earning anything at all.

The leaderboard that decides the airdrop locks one week before early access, which lands in November or December. Between now and then the lines on our page will only move upwards, because everyone else is still depositing. That is why the planner asks how much headroom you want: aiming at exactly today’s line is a plan to be overtaken.

What we still can’t tell you

  • Your official rank. Only Yakkamon knows that, because only Yakkamon can see the off-chain points.
  • Where the 5,000th place actually sits. Not enough people have deposited to draw that line from chain data.
  • Which addresses belong to the same person. One trainer, one deposit address is the assumption. It is a reasonable one, and it is still an assumption.
  • Whether deposits will run the full window. They are scheduled to close on 16 November, but the multiplier reaches its floor four weeks before that — the cheapest points are the ones you buy today.

Have a look

The board is at yakkamonworld.com/leaderboard, it recalculates from both chains through the day, and it is free and needs no account. If you find something in it that looks wrong, tell us — a public number that nobody checks is worth less than no number at all.
