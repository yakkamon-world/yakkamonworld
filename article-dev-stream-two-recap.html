Second Yakkamon Dev Stream: The Airdrop U-Turn Explained, and a Free Mint That May Move to September

Dev stream: second Yakkamon broadcast · Published Aug 14, 2026

Thought Farm ran their second Yakkamon-only stream, with Adam, Craig, Spencer and Bryn all on the mic for about an hour. It opened with an apology, closed with a request for more memes, and in between it moved two dates, put a number on how much money is sitting in the deposit pool, and explained for the first time what your monsters will actually do.

Here's everything that matters, ordered by how much it should change what you're doing this week.

1. The airdrop change: the team's own account of it

Adam went straight at it. He described being put to the sword by the player base over the reward table update, and put the blame on the announcement rather than the change: he did a poor job writing it and conveying what had actually happened.

The reasoning behind the change was simple. Sign-ups exploded, and the top of the leaderboard started moving fast as deposits came in. So the team did two things: extended a solid reward down to the top 2,000 instead of the top 1,000, and added a brand new Legendary above the existing top tier as a thank-you to the players who had committed the most.

His summary of the outcome: in every case you are still getting the same monster, and in a lot of cases you're now getting something better.

And the band that caused the confusion is confirmed as Legendary. Adam said directly that the rare tier running up to 2,000 supply will be a Legendary as well — because the feedback made clear players wanted a real shot at one. That is exactly the reading our correction settled on after we initially got it wrong, and it's now confirmed out loud rather than inferred from a table.

What it means for you: nothing to do. If you were at rank 1,500 and panicking on the 12th, you weren't demoted, and the utility attached to your band went up rather than down. The panic was a communication failure, not a reward cut.

2. What “Legendary” and “Rare” actually mean — and why it matters

This is new information, and it's more consequential than it sounds. The team explained the internal definitions:

TierWhat defines it
LegendaryScarcity. Pre-minted, supply-limited, fixed. Once the supply of that monster exists, that's all there will ever be.
RareTime. Catchable only during limited windows. The supply isn't fixed — it depends on how much players hunt during that window.

Then the detail worth circling: Legendaries will have traits that only Legendaries can have. Not a bigger number on the same trait — abilities no other monster gets access to.

This doesn't contradict the “same power level” framing. That framing was about the Genesis Legendaries relative to each other — Storm, Echo, Bloom and Tide differ by species and role, not strength. What's new is the gap between Legendary and Rare, which is now clearly a capability difference and not just a scarcity label.

Full utilities for each Genesis monster land in September. Until then the team's own request is to wait rather than trade on assumptions — they're still tuning drop rates, yields and even the art.

3. The free mint may move to mid-September

This is the single biggest scheduling item in the stream, and it wasn't framed as an announcement — it came out while Adam was answering a question about the leaderboard lock. The sequence he described:

StepTiming given on stream
Ronin free mint“Likely” mid-September
Mint revealMid-October
Leaderboard finalisedAfter the reveal — weeks, a month, possibly longer
Rewards distributed, then early accessQ4 2026 (internal date exists, not shared)
Update, 14 August — this one landed. The official docs have since moved the free mint to mid-September, with the reveal in mid-October. The caution below was written before that page went up. The published schedule →
Treat this as a signal, not a new date. The published date is still October 1st, and Adam said “likely” rather than announcing a change. But if the mint does land in mid-September, every verification deadline you've mentally filed under “end of September” moves up by roughly two weeks. Get your Discord and X verification done now rather than in late September — it costs you nothing to be early, and being late costs you the mint entirely.

Two more mint details came out of the FAQ round. The supply is no longer firmly 10,000: the team is working with Ronin and it may land between 10,000 and 15,000. And the whitelist will deliberately hold more addresses than there are NFTs — possibly 20–30% over capacity in the early waves — because a wave that doesn't mint out looks bad. Later waves are explicitly not restricted to top-ranked trainers.

What it means for you: if you're an ordinary trainer with a modest point total, the free mint remains the most accessible NFT in the whole programme. It's the one reward where being early and verified matters more than being rich.

4. The leaderboard lock finally has a shape

We've been flagging for two weeks that the lock had no announced date. It still doesn't have one — but it now has a position in the sequence, which is nearly as useful.

The leaderboard runs until the game launches, ending roughly a week before early access begins, possibly a little less. It will be finalised after the mint reveal, and the team says it will announce a specific end date in advance so trainers can prepare.

The practical read: you have more runway than the word “locks” suggested — likely into late Q4. But two things still bite. You must hold your rank until the lock, and the leaderboard goes dark as launch approaches. A late sprint is a plan you can't measure.

5. 2.5 million $FLOWER is already in the deposit pool

Craig put a number on deposits for the first time, and it's a big one. Roughly 2.5 million $FLOWER has been deposited into Yakkamon so far — which he estimated at a fifth to a quarter of the circulating $FLOWER in Sunflower Land.

Adam's response was that the team consistently underestimates how hard players will push, and that this level of commitment is both motivating and slightly terrifying on the delivery side.

What it means for you: the paid bracket at the top of the leaderboard is being contested harder than most people assume, and points-per-rank at the top will keep inflating. It doesn't change the free route — the top 100,000 early-access cutoff is still comfortably reachable without spending — but if you were eyeing a Legendary band on deposits alone, the price of a given rank is rising every week.

6. OG status: the exact requirement, and how fast it gets harder

OG has a precise number now. It's 50,000 points inside a single week, and Craig gave the current cost: about 15,000 $FLOWER this week at the 3× multiplier.

It's a weekly milestone, and it's permanent once claimed — if you hit it this week and never come close again, you're still OG. And because the multiplier decays, the $FLOWER cost of that same 50,000 points climbs every week:

WeekMultiplierRoughly what OG costs in one transfer
1 (now)3.0×~15,000 $FLOWER
22.8×~15,600
42.4×~17,900
62.0×~20,800
11+1.0×~35,700

Our calculation, assuming a single transfer in the +40% size-bonus band. Craig's 15,000 figure for week one matches this maths. Check your own deposit panel before acting on it.

One deliberate design note: the team discussed attaching a large reward to OG and decided against it, settling on a title only — specifically to avoid incentivising the wrong behaviour and setting expectations of some enormous payout. If you were depositing for OG on the assumption a prize is coming later, that assumption has now been explicitly denied.

7. The multiplier may never actually reach 1.0×

A sharp question from chat: if the leaderboard locks before the multiplier finishes decaying, does the schedule change? Craig's answer was that the rate simply keeps stepping down weekly — and he acknowledged, apparently for the first time, that deposits may close while it's still around 1.2×. If it does reach 1.0×, it stays there rather than dropping further.

What it means for you: nothing about the advice changes — earlier is still worth more, always. But the tail end of our published schedule may be theoretical. There may be no such thing as a 1.0× deposit week.

8. They would rather you spent that $FLOWER than withdrew it

Adam was refreshingly direct about a problem the team can see coming: a large amount of $FLOWER has been parked in Yakkamon by people who can simply withdraw it at launch.

Their answer is carrots rather than sticks. Expect discounts on hard-currency items, VIP offers, hunting events and possibly auctions during the opening weeks of early access, designed to give you something worth spending on before you think about withdrawing.

Read this next to the withdrawal limits. Percentage-based limits already apply during the first few weeks after launch. Now add a wave of limited-time offers landing in exactly that window. If your plan was “deposit for points, withdraw on day one,” expect the game to be actively designed to change your mind — and expect the exit to be metered while it tries.

9. What your monsters actually do — the answer to “why collect these?”

Bryn, the lead game designer, spent the middle of the stream on this, and it's the best explanation of the collection loop we've had so far.

  • Monsters are workers, and they're tradable. The team is positioning them as the equivalent of the big chapter items in Sunflower Land — the things that define a build.
  • Traits are randomly rolled on a percentage chance, and rares and legendaries carry significant farming buffs. Which monsters you hold determines how you build your farm.
  • Genetics and base stats are real systems. Breed two of the same species with good stats and traits and you carry that line forward. Bryn described deliberately designing for one-to-three-year breeding projects, including sneaky routes to move traits between species and between type groups.
  • Breeding degrades, but isn't capped. Keep breeding from the same monster and random noise increases, so results get progressively worse. There's no hard limit on attempts.
  • RNG is bounded. Bryn doesn't like stacking RNG on RNG — if you understand the system you should be able to predict an outcome within 80–90% and plan best and worst cases.
  • Evolutions are planned. Confirmed as something they're interested in and designing for.

On what becomes an NFT — and Bryn was careful to say this isn't a promise — the current structure is Legendaries as NFTs (close to one-of-ones or extremely scarce), Rares as SFTs, and commons as plain in-game assets. Adam added that this is deliberate: not every monster becomes an NFT, precisely so the NFTs stay rare and worth trading.

And there's a sink. Contract deliveries for monsters will consume commons using progression coins — described as “hungrier” than your generation rate — so the common population gets burned rather than inflating endlessly.

What it means for you: rank isn't just a trophy. A Genesis Legendary is a permanent, buff-bearing, breedable asset with traits nobody else can obtain, entering an economy explicitly designed to keep it scarce. That's the strongest case yet for the points you're grinding now.

10. A new idea: export limits tied to how much you actually play

Bryn floated a concept — his words were that it's an idea, not a system yet — for handling extractive players without Sunflower Land's hard limits. Your export limit would grow with your in-game activity: play and burn resources legitimately and your ability to trade out expands, while someone farming purely to extract hits the ceiling almost immediately. VIP accounts would be treated as legitimate and uncapped.

Very early, explicitly beta, and aimed at one of the genuinely hard problems in web3 gaming. Worth watching rather than planning around.

11. Gameplay: the first two hours are being built now

Spencer is building the vertical slice — roughly the first couple of hours of the game. How it works:

You start on a small revealed patch of world with your first monster. It bounces around gathering berries and wood. You use what it gathers to part the clouds and reveal more land, and some of that new land contains hunting areas. The hunting mechanic isn't locked yet, but the shape is: you send a monster in with lures to try and draw wild monsters back to your land, and you can go in and look around yourself. Sometimes an aggressive monster turns up and challenges you to a battle.

A short clip did make it out on X during the stream — a monster (Spencer used the name “Wadler”, unconfirmed) moving around gathering resources, with one expansion already revealing a lumber yard and a hunting area.

Also confirmed, with some amusement: the monster art that leaked earlier came through a developer backdoor used to avoid testing on production. Someone found it. That backdoor is now closed.

12. The sound is being generated with AI, and they said so up front

Craig has taken on audio in his first week on Yakkamon, and volunteered without being asked that he's using AI to generate the game's sounds. His reasoning: stock libraries can't deliver the specificity they want — his example was trying to find an existing sound that captures a chunky, Humpty-Dumpty-ish mouse monster.

Music, ambient beds and effects all exist already, though a technical hitch meant none of it could be played on stream. The plan is per-monster voices and personalities, with shared sounds for generic actions by body type — and one delightful production idea of recording the team making noises into a mic and modulating them per monster. Bryn: every time a monster faints, it's one of them screaming.

Community sounds are on the table, provided they match the recording fidelity and characteristics of the rest of the game. And the audio tech is planned to be back-ported into Sunflower Land.

13. Why Discord went quiet: a three-day restriction

If you noticed the Discord acting strangely, here's why. The bot ban waves cleaned up thousands of low-intent accounts on the Yakkamon side, but not before those accounts hit Discord — which placed a roughly three-day restriction on the server. Images couldn't be posted and invite links stopped working, which is also why this stream had almost no visual leaks. The restriction was expected to lift at the end of the day. Discord support hadn't responded.

Adam's framing is worth repeating, because it explains the run of rule changes: the tightening of verification and point rules isn't arbitrary — it's protecting the economy and, at this point, the server itself.

14. Quick answers from the FAQ round

  • Can Storm and Echo be caught in-game later? A very small amount goes into the rewards pool, but the overwhelming majority only ever come from the trainer airdrop. Occasional special events may release a few more.
  • Platform: browser web app at launch. No mobile app, no console. Not ruled out forever, but not planned.
  • Free to play: confirmed. No NFT and no wallet needed to play; the web3 layer is opt-in.
  • Changing your linked wallet: being looked into after several people linked the wrong one. No ETA.
  • In-game marketplace: confirmed. Monster NFTs mint on Ronin before early access and can be traded on the Ronin marketplace immediately; once early access opens you deposit them into the game and can use the in-game market too.
  • Seasonal resets: not happening. Bryn is against them — mistakes get fixed rather than wiped. Inflation gets handled with hard sinks and new content, including an infinite XP sink of the kind Sunflower Land just shipped.
  • Open source: the client will be open sourced. Adam credits AI tooling for the recent surge in community contributions — 30 community features, patches and fixes shipped to Sunflower Land in seven days.
  • Monster names: not finalised. Bryn wants a naming framework that makes them feel distinctly Yakkamon, but is open to community suggestions inside it — and the team is watching the memes, because they're shaping the personalities. Meme creators, in Adam's words, have more influence than they think.
  • Discord points: Adam said they're not doing Discord points any more, offering “personal points” for memes instead. Whether this cancels the manual weekly reward mentioned after the first stream wasn't spelled out.

What we're changing on this site

Two things need flagging honestly. The free mint date is published across this site as October 1st, and it still is officially — but a mid-September mint was spoken about as likely, so we'll be carrying that as a caveat until the team confirms one way or the other. And our deposit multiplier schedule runs to a 1.0× floor in week 11; that floor may never arrive if deposits close first, so the table gets a note.

Everything else in this stream confirmed rather than contradicted what's here — including, gratifyingly, the Legendary status of the 501–2,000 band that our correction hinged on.

Verification is the deadline that moved

If the mint lands in mid-September, being verified late costs you the whole thing.
